# Provider Types Guide

Boston's education landscape includes seven distinct provider types. This guide explains what each one means so you can decide which is the best fit for your child and family.

---

## Boston Public School

**Ages/Grades:** Pre-K (K0) through Grade 12 (up to age 22 for students with an IEP)
**Cost:** Free
**Count in database:** 111 schools

Boston Public Schools (BPS) is the city's public school district, operating 119 schools including early education centers, elementary schools, Pre-K–8 schools, and high schools. BPS offers a standards-based education in settings that value diversity and inclusion, with individualized instruction and family partnerships.

Within the district you'll find several specialized models:

- **Exam Schools** — Three selective schools (Boston Latin School, Boston Latin Academy, O'Bryant) that admit students through competitive entrance exams.
- **Pilot Schools** — 19 autonomous schools with flexibility in hiring, budgeting, and curriculum (e.g., Boston Arts Academy, TechBoston Academy).
- **Horace Mann Charter Schools** — Six semi-autonomous, state-approved in-district charter schools funded by BPS.
- **Innovation Schools** — Schools with operational flexibility, often created to turn around underperforming programs.
- **Dual Language Programs** — Nine schools teaching in English plus a partner language (Spanish, Mandarin, Japanese, or French).
- **International Baccalaureate Programs** — Rigorous, globally-focused college-prep curricula at select schools.

**Best for:** Families looking for a free, structured public education with a wide range of program options from Pre-K through high school, including specialized and advanced academic tracks.

**More info:** [BPS School Types](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/schools-container/school-types)

## Family Child Care

**Ages:** Infants through preschool (typically 0–5 years)
**Cost:** Varies; many accept CCFA (Child Care Financial Assistance)
**Count in database:** 685 providers

Family Child Care providers are licensed individuals who care for small groups of 6–10 children of mixed ages in their own homes. They are licensed by the Massachusetts Department of Early Education and Care (EEC).

Key characteristics:

- **Home-like setting** with small group sizes and a single consistent caregiver.
- **Flexible hours** — many offer non-traditional schedules (early mornings, evenings).
- **Mixed ages** — siblings of different ages can be placed together.
- **Culturally responsive** — providers often reflect the cultural and linguistic backgrounds of the families they serve.

**Best for:** Families seeking a warm, home-like environment with a consistent caregiver, flexible scheduling, and the ability to keep siblings of different ages together.

**More info:** [BPS Great Starts](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/enrollment/greatstarts-container/greatstarts)

## Community Based Organization

**Ages:** Primarily infants through pre-school age (0–5 years)
**Cost:** Varies; many are free through Head Start or UPK; many accept CCFA
**Count in database:** 195 programs

Community Based Organizations (CBOs) are nonprofits and community agencies that offer early childhood education and care. Many participate in Boston's Universal Pre-K (UPK) program and federally-funded Head Start. Well-known Boston CBOs include ABCD (Action for Boston Community Development), Nurtury, the YMCA of Greater Boston, and Catholic Charities.

Key characteristics:

- **Mission-driven** — run by nonprofits focused on community needs rather than profit.
- **Head Start programs** — many CBOs operate Head Start and Early Head Start, which provide free comprehensive early education to income-eligible families.
- **Wraparound services** — often include family support, health screenings, and referrals beyond just education.
- **Language and cultural programming** — many serve multilingual communities.

**Best for:** Families looking for affordable or free early education with strong community ties and comprehensive family support services, especially those eligible for Head Start or seeking culturally and linguistically responsive programs.

**More info:** [Boston Early Childhood](https://www.boston.gov/departments/early-childhood), [ABCD Head Start](https://bostonabcd.org/service/head-start-childrens-services/)

## Center-Based Child Care, Independent Preschool

**Ages:** Infants (under 15 months), toddlers (15 months–2 years 9 months), preschoolers (2 years 9 months to kindergarten entry)
**Cost:** Tuition-based (average private preschool tuition in Boston is ~$32,000/year); some accept CCFA
**Count in database:** 3 providers

These are licensed facilities that provide group care in dedicated commercial or institutional settings, organized into classrooms by age group with multiple staff members. They are licensed by the Massachusetts Department of Early Education and Care (EEC) and can be operated by individual owners, for-profit companies, nonprofits, or faith-based organizations.

Key characteristics:

- **Structured, classroom-like environment** with age-specific curricula and activities.
- **Wider variety of equipment, supplies, and activities** compared to home-based settings.
- **Multiple staff members** per classroom with specialized training.
- **Consistent schedule** aligned with traditional school hours.

**Best for:** Families wanting a structured classroom environment with dedicated facilities, diverse enrichment activities, and peer interaction grouped by age, and who are prepared to pay tuition.

**More info:** [BPS Great Starts](https://www.bostonpublicschools.org/enrollment/greatstarts-container/greatstarts)

## Independent School

**Ages:** Varies by school (preschool through Grade 12)
**Cost:** Tuition-based (average private school tuition in Boston is ~$35,000/year)
**Count in database:** 23 schools

Independent schools are privately funded institutions that set their own curricula, admissions standards, and operational policies. They are not part of the public school system and charge tuition. Some participate in Boston's Universal Pre-K (UPK) program for their pre-kindergarten offerings.

Key characteristics:

- **Curricular freedom** — can offer specialized pedagogies (e.g., Montessori, Waldorf, religious, classical).
- **Selective admissions** — may have application processes and entrance requirements.
- **Smaller class sizes** — typically lower student-to-teacher ratios than public schools.
- **Private funding** — funded through tuition, endowments, and fundraising rather than public dollars.

**Best for:** Families seeking a specific educational philosophy, smaller class sizes, or specialized programming, and who have the financial means (or financial aid) to cover tuition.

## Charter School

**Ages/Grades:** Varies by school (typically K–12)
**Cost:** Free (publicly funded)
**Count in database:** 8 schools

Charter schools are independent public schools that operate under five-year charters granted by the Massachusetts Board of Elementary and Secondary Education. They are tuition-free and open to all students, with admission by lottery when demand exceeds capacity. Massachusetts has two types: **Commonwealth Charters** (independent of any district) and **Horace Mann Charters** (located within a school district like BPS).

Key characteristics:

- **Operational autonomy** — free to design their own curriculum, manage budgets, and hire/fire staff independently.
- **Accountability** — must demonstrate strong results within five years or risk losing their charter.
- **Lottery-based enrollment** — cannot impose selective entrance requirements; students are admitted by random lottery.
- **Innovative models** — often organized around a specific mission, theme, or teaching method.

**Best for:** Families who want a free public education but are drawn to a specific school mission, teaching approach, or smaller school community that differs from the traditional district model.

**More info:** [MA DESE Charter Schools](https://www.doe.mass.edu/charter/about.html)

## Independent Special Education School

**Ages:** 3–21 years
**Cost:** Typically paid by the referring public school district (free to families)
**Count in database:** 2 schools

Independent Special Education Schools (sometimes called "Chapter 766 schools" or "766 Approved Private Schools") are state-approved private schools designed for students whose educational needs cannot be met within their local public school. They are approved and monitored by the Massachusetts Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (DESE) through the Office of Approved Special Education Schools (OASES).

Key characteristics:

- **Specialized programming** — designed to meet unique learning, social, and emotional needs across many types of disabilities (e.g., autism, dyslexia, visual/hearing impairments).
- **IEP-driven placement** — students are typically placed through the IEP (Individualized Education Program) process when the public school determines it cannot provide an appropriate education.
- **Publicly funded** — tuition is paid by the student's home school district or other referring agency, not by families.
- **State oversight** — monitored on a six-year cycle by DESE for compliance with state and federal special education law.

**Best for:** Families of children with significant special needs whose IEP team has determined that a specialized setting outside the public school district is necessary to provide an appropriate education.

**More info:** [DESE Office of Approved Special Education Schools](https://www.doe.mass.edu/oases/), [MAAPS](https://maaps.org/)

## Quick Comparison

| Provider Type | Cost | Ages/Grades | Setting | Enrollment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Boston Public School** | Free | Pre-K – Grade 12 | School buildings | District assignment/application |
| **Family Child Care** | Varies (CCFA accepted) | 0–5 years | Provider's home | Direct with provider |
| **Community Based Organization** | Free or low-cost | 0–5 years | Community centers | Application/lottery |
| **Center-Based Child Care** | Tuition-based | 0–5 years | Dedicated facility | Direct with center |
| **Independent School** | Tuition-based (~$35K/yr) | Pre-K – Grade 12 | Private campus | Selective application |
| **Charter School** | Free | K–12 | School buildings | Lottery |
| **Independent Special Education** | Free (district-funded) | 3–21 years | Specialized facility | IEP team placement |
